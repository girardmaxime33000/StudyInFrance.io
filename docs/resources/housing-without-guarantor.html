Housing

Finding housing in France without a French guarantor

Why the guarantor requirement trips up new arrivals

Most private landlords in France ask for a "garant" — someone based in France who commits to paying your rent if you can't. It's a reasonable ask for a French tenant with family nearby; it's a real obstacle for someone arriving from India with no local contacts. The good news: this requirement has well-established workarounds most landlords already accept.

1. Guarantor-substitute schemes

Several state-backed and private schemes act as your guarantor for a fee or for free, and are widely recognized by landlords:

  • Visale — a free, state-backed guarantee available to students under a certain age, covering unpaid rent for the landlord. Widely accepted, especially by student residences.
  • Private paid guarantor services that issue a certificate landlords treat like a traditional guarantor letter, for a percentage of annual rent.

2. Where to look, in order of guarantor-friendliness

  • CROUS and private student residences — built around Visale and student-specific paperwork, the easiest starting point.
  • Purpose-built student housing operators (private residences aimed at international students) — usually accept Visale or their own in-house guarantee.
  • Private landlords via listing sites — the hardest without a scheme above, but not impossible; being upfront about using Visale from the first message saves everyone time.

3. Vocabulary that avoids confusion at signing

A few terms that trip up first-time renters in France: the dépôt de garantie (security deposit, usually one month's rent) is separate from your first month's rent, both typically due before you get keys. The état des lieux is a formal move-in inspection report — read it carefully, since its condition notes are what your deposit is checked against when you move out.

4. Red flags that mean a listing is a scam

  • A landlord who is "traveling abroad" and asks for a deposit by wire transfer before any viewing, in person or by video
  • Rent significantly below the going rate for the area with no clear explanation
  • Pressure to decide and pay within hours, before any documentation
  • Refusal to do a live video call to show the actual apartment

If any of these appear, stop the conversation — a legitimate listing survives you taking a day to verify it.
